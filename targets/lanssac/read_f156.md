Where pass 3 departs from the decipherer: he wrote "si monsieur estoit ennemy d'icy de la France"; the cipher, with the
stacked 8 as *st* (Tomokiyo) and the clover as *-nt*, reads "se monstrent estre ennemis jurez de … France" — the German
princes, not Monsieur. Pass 2's "mon frere" (from a supposed *f* value) is withdrawn.

Sense: Lanssac fears that the Emperor will forestall them and that the King of Denmark, bending to the Emperor's will,
could make trouble at the Sound, less than half a league wide and fortified on both sides; going by land would put
Anjou at the mercy of the German princes, who show themselves sworn enemies of France and of Anjou and are extremely
sorry to see themselves shut in between two such powerful kings; the road by Italy and Dalmatia is too long and costly;
the Polish lords will ask him to bring 4,000 Gascons; Catherine should gather all the money she can so that when the
embassy comes to bring the crown she is not then embarrassed to find it, should reckon on the ships being fit for
embarking and have the galleys made ready. Of the Polish lords only four are against them; the whole nobility cries
out for a king, and namely wants Anjou.
