# "Letters not deciphered": SP 95/136 (Sweden, 1748-55) and the marquis del Puerto intercept, SP 87/24/33 (1748)

Status: blocked (offline only: neither volume is digitised free; images only through Gale's State Papers Online or a
TNA copy order. Closed on 28 Sept 2026 at Daniel's choice, with the keys located; reopen when images are in hand.)

Session of 28 September 2026. Catalogue entry 345. Goal set by Daniel: solve it.

## What the target is

- **TNA SP 95/136**, "Supplementary: Letters not deciphered", 1748-1755 (Discovery C3189519). Its series, SP 95
  (State Papers Foreign, Sweden), is mainly in-letters from British envoys at Stockholm. Discovery gives no item-level
  description, no extent and `digitised: false`; the volume has no children in the catalogue.
- **TNA SP 87/24/33**, ff. 96-97: "Undeciphered letter from the marquis del Puerto, Spanish ambassador to The
  Hague", 19 March 1748 (Discovery C9072127, `digitised: false`). SP 87 is State Papers Foreign, Military
  Expeditions (the Duke of Cumberland's papers, Low Countries campaign): a letter taken during the Breda/Aix
  negotiations.

**Correction to the catalogue premise.** The SP 95 series description (Discovery C13634) says there was "a complete
rupture of diplomatic relations between Britain and Sweden ... between 1748 and 1763". SP 95/136 therefore cannot
be a British envoy's despatches from Stockholm: there was no envoy. It must hold intercepts (Swedish letters opened in
the British or Hanoverian posts) or letters of unofficial British correspondents in Sweden. The catalogue's reading
("the Deciphering Branch intercepts it failed on") is plausible but unconfirmed until the volume is seen. "Not
deciphered" may also mean only that no decipherment was filed with the copy (the same caution as for
Cryptiana's "undeciphered": a filing statement, not proof that the office failed).

## The keys exist: the Deciphering Branch's own key sheets on DECODE (BL Add MS 32253-32310)

Searched DECODE's list for BL Deciphering Branch key records dated 1744-1760 and opened the ones for Sweden and
Spain with the project cookie (images in the scratchpad, not committed; BL images are not public domain).

Spanish (BL Add MS 32301):
- **R7801, f. 38**: docket (image P1) "Grimaldi to Canis at Stockholm, 1752. & Marq. del Puerto at the Hague". Image
  P2 is the key the Branch rebuilt: a Spanish syllabic nomenclator numbered 1-500 (seen filled to about 460), syllables
  and words (*contra, correo, corte, despues, interesse, intelligen-, Ingalaterra, Hanover, Viena, S.M.Xma*,
  *Su Magestad Brit.*), many numbers followed by a string of lower-case letters (e.g. "20 - a.b.f.h.i.m.o"), which
  look like the letter suffixes the Branch saw attached to that number. **So by 1752 the Branch read del Puerto's
  correspondence.** Whether the 1748 letter in SP 87/24/33 is in this key, an earlier one, or one of the 1747 keys
  below is the first thing to test once the image is in hand.
- **R7793, f. 28**: headed "Huescar with Wall 1747" (read from the rotated heading of image P1); a Spanish
  code of 1-1100 (e.g. 601 *ell*, 603 *Parma*, 761 *Newcastle*, 768 *plenipotenciario*, 913 *Toscana*, 973
  *Suecia*). Huéscar was Spanish ambassador in Paris and Ricardo Wall envoy in London in 1747-48, during the peace
  talks that also involved del Puerto at The Hague.
- R7794 (f. 29, Wall 1747), R7795 (f. 31, Carvajal 1747), R7796-R7800 (ff. 32-37, Wall, Ensenada 1749-50),
  R7802-R7809 (ff. 39-49, Grimaldi, Abreu, Massones 1752-55): the rest of the Spanish run, not opened.

Swedish (BL Add MS 32287):
- **R7721, ff. 97-98**: docket "Wynantz avec Scheffer 1749". Image P2 is a code of at least 500 groups mixing French
  and Swedish (1 *up*, 3 *marche*, 45 *penningar*, 35 *sina*, 36 *mycket*, 211 *Stockholm*, 313 *Copenhague*, 322
  *walpole*, 374 *Riks-rad*, 474 *Chesterf.*, 480 *Carteret*).
- **R7723, ff. 101-106**: "Scheffer" 1752, twelve images, a code running past 1400 (P1: 1001-1200; P2: 1201-1400), with
  groups declared *nulle*.
- R7722 (ff. 99-100, "B. de Scheffer"), R7724 (ff. 107-108, Wynantz 1753), R7725 (ff. 109-110, Wynantz): further
  Swedish keys; R7724 P1 and R7725 P1 are blank covers or dockets only (R7725 docketed "Wynantz").
- Carl Fredrik Scheffer was Swedish minister in Paris 1744-52. So the Branch broke the Swedish diplomatic codes of
  1749, 1752 and 1753; SP 95/136 may hold letters in other Swedish systems of those years, or letters it filed without
  a decipherment although the key existed.
