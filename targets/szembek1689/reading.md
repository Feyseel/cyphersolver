# Reading: Latin cipher letter in the Szembek papers (Biblioteka Kórnicka BK 1560, WBC "List zaszyfrowany")

with the key in `key.tsv` (rebuilt from the contemporary interlinear glosses and checked run by run by

## Translation

P.S. The letter written in your own hand was received with a grateful heart, both by His Highness the Prince (who
salutes Your Most Reverend Lordship with due veneration) and by me and the others whom it concerned. And as we have
so far proceeded sincerely in everything, so now too we proceed, and will proceed, according to a right Christian
conscience, although some Christians do not behave so and do not proceed with us as they had promised and had urged
us in many ways. But this we leave to divine Justice and Providence, which knows and sees all things. Oh! my Lord,
Most Reverend Father, if you heard how great and what kind of things, and by what means, that impostor and detractor,
the Burgundian Antide Dunod, without conscience, second to none in the world for importunity and chatter, together
with his devoted companion, a perfidious traitor, [...] under [202], caused to us and to this afflicted country; and
how he led astray that Prince of pious memory, the late Şerban, who, believing their lies and deceptions wrapped in
fine words and rejecting the counsel of all others, entangled us and this wretched country in great difficulties. O
if you understood the promises that they brought from that court to the late Prince and carried back from here,
which now, through their wicked reports, have been found for the most part false! Truly, some would move one to
laughter, some would cause wonder, and some ought to be wrapped in silence for shame. May God most just take
vengeance on them according to their works and their conscience.

Of Gegenbey, know that some time ago he was killed through the treachery of a false friend of his, and his head sent
as a gift. But the companion of this same Gegenbey, named Gityk, is in fact more active than he was, in the East.

## Notes on the reading

- *Antidius Dunod*: Antide Dunod SJ, a Jesuit from Franche-Comté ("Burgundus"), Emperor Leopold I's envoy who, with
  Count László Csáky, pressed Şerban Cantacuzino of Wallachia (prince 1678-1688) in 1687-1688 to join the Holy League.
- *defunctum Principem Serbanum*: Şerban Cantacuzino, who died on 29 October 1688. The letter is therefore later than
  that date. "Celsissimus Princeps", the writer's living master, is then most likely his successor Constantin
  Brâncoveanu (1688-1714), and "illa aula" the imperial court in Vienna. This identification is inferred from the
  text, not stated in it.
- *Gegenbeio / Gegenbeii*: the cipher spells Gegenbe(i)-, the glosser wrote "Gergenbeio" and "Gergenbeii". Not
  identified (perhaps a "bey"). *Gityk*: not identified.
- "Damis(?)": the clear word before "sub 202" is a capital D or P + "amis"; not resolved. The marginal "+" on this
  line in the same ink suggests a contemporary reader also stopped here.
