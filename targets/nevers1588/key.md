Code numbers (plain, uncoded figures in the text): 9 = duc de Guise, 10 = Épernon, 72 = Savoie, 73 = (Savoy's) ambassadeur
(all glossed on f. 131, 2 Oct 2026; 10 also on f. 139), 11 = le Roy, 24 = the duc de Nevers (both glossed on f. 131; the
gloss over 24 reads "nevers", first misread here as Navarre, corrected 22 Sept 2026 from ff. 133-134, `targets/r3708/`);
102 = Paris (from context); 40 with m above = 40 000.

The cells with several values are what the transcription and the readings support. Some may be one glyph misread
as another in this hand (2/z, 8/d, 9/g).

Segmentation note (2 Oct 2026, f. 131 full reading): a bare "1" = i. What the table listed as the two-character signs
"1n" and "1e" is bare 1 (i) followed by n (l) or e (epsilon, t): f. 131 "que s'il perissoit" = h s 13 1 n 16 t 14 p 13 f
17 1 E, and on f. 62 "failly" = 4 2 1 n o y, "avoit" = ... k 1 e, "remueroit" = ... 17 1 e. The f. 62 readings do not
change; only the unit boundaries do. 1d (n), 1s (c/qu), 1b (p), 1r (g) and the numbers 11-19 remain two-character signs.
