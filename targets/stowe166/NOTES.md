# Stowe MS 166: Edmondes to Burghley, 1592–1594 (DECODE R7770–R7775)

Status: in progress
Reading so far: read in part
Prior decipherment in print (found 30 Sept 2026, after the key was recovered here): G. G. Butler, *The Edmondes
Papers* (1913), prints five of the six letters with the cipher passages deciphered. See "Print check" below.

Catalogue entry: "Lord Threr? to unknown recipient, 5 ciphertexts". DECODE's author field is the
**addressee**: the letters are to the Lord Treasurer (Burghley). Stowe MS 166 is volume 1 of the papers of
**Thomas Edmondes**, Elizabeth's agent with Henri IV (BL catalogue: "Papers of Thomas Edmondes, Agent at the
French Court, 1592-1596"). The letters open "It may please yor L." and report the King's words to the writer
first-hand ("as it pleaseth himself to tell me", "the K. thereuppon saide vnto me"), dated from the King's
camps (Blois, Tours, the camp before Laon). They are Edmondes's despatches, or copies of them. The DECODE "sender"
values (John le Rios, John Symmons, "Permell of Dieppo", "One of Dieppe") come from later dockets and endorsements.
They do not match the hand or the content. R7771 (ff. 56–57) is part of the same group.

Dates corrected 30 Sept 2026. Edmondes wrote the English year, which began on 25 March, so "3 Mar 1592" on f. 59
is 3 March 1593 in modern reckoning. Evidence: Butler 1913 prints ff. 50–61 under 19 January, 16 February, 3 March
and 16 March 1593 (his running heads). The BL Stowe catalogue (1895) starts the volume on 18 June 1592, and its f. 27
is Burghley's letter of 26 Sept 1592 promising Edmondes a cypher from Unton, so ff. 50–61 fall after that.
Edmondes's "Mantes the 12th of January 1593" (Butler no. LIX) is January 1594. The bracketed dates of R7770 and
R7775 are Butler's. The earlier "Jan–Mar 1592" dates took the year as written.

## The cipher

A simple substitution of English, word divisions kept, with a few homophones and some variant letter forms.
There are also single code signs for persons. It was broken from the words ω4ʋ = the, α8 = of and ωα = to,
with "difficulties" and "empeachement" as the first long words. Same key in all six records, 1593 to 1594
(dated 1592–1594 on the leaves), and two different hands.

| sign | = | sign | = | sign | = |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| δ | a | б / 6 | g (also a) | + | b |
| 2 | c | ш | d, u/v | ʋ | e |
| 8 | f | 4 | h | ǂ | i |
| 7 | k | 3 | l | □ | m |
| ȝ | n | α | o | 9 | p |
| f | r | & | s | ω | t |
| o (small) | v | π | w | 5 | x |
| λ | y (q?) | | | | |

Code signs: **9** (standing alone) = a person, most likely the Count of Soissons in 1593 (R7770, R7772);
**n** = the new suitor for Catherine de Bourbon (R7770), unidentified; **£ / £2** = most likely the Duke of Guise in 1594
(Champagne and Reims in R7774, Provence against Épernon in R7775); **4̇** unexplained.
Print evidence, 30 Sept 2026 (see "Print check"): Love (2001) quotes the f. 50 sentence as "great hope, that he
[i.e., Montpensier] will render himself of the Religion", and names Soissons as the suitor the King meant to stop.
So n = the Duke of Montpensier by Love's gloss (M until Butler's page is read). Butler's p. 46 (f. 50) has
"Soissons" twice, and R7770 has 9 twice. His p. 150 (f. 112) has "Guise". But his p. 56 (f. 58v) has no "Soissons",
so the "9:" before the f. 58v passage (and perhaps before f. 57's) may be a mark rather than the person sign.

## Readings

**R7770, f. 50** (after "the K. continueth his resolution to goe to Saumur to see his sister, not so much for
that cause (as it pleaseth himself to tell me) as especiallye"):
> [4̇] to breake of the marriage betwene her and [9] wch he tould me he is resolued not to permitt but to geive
> her vnto [n] of whom he said he hath great hope that he will render himself of the religion. [9] is herewth
> enraged and extreemelie discontented.

**R7771, f. 57** (after "…by the Pope, will appeare vnto yor L. by the enclosed"):
> [9]: is glad to here of, hoping to be no further troubled wth the clamor of the Catholikes for the seekinge of him.

**R7772, f. 58v** (after "…had gonne forwarder, whereupon this preuention"):
> [9]: I doe secretelie learne that Monsr Montmorancy hath made great instance, as a condition whereof he
> required the performance before he would come, to haue the yong prince of Condé deliuered into the handes of
> Madame d'Angolesme his sister in law to be vnder his custodie.

**R7772, f. 59**, 4 lines: **not read.** Only fragments: "…papistes…", "…to the place…of the constable (?)…
of the …", ending "…the crowne, but wherein he hath nothing effected, the K. being resolued not to assent thereunto" (the last part in clear).
30 Sept 2026: Butler prints this letter whole (no. XXII, from ff. 58, 59). His p. 56 holds the f. 58v passage and the
clear ending of f. 59 ("wherein", "nothing", "resolued", "assent", "thereunto"), so these four lines are on that page
too. The page's words that fit the fragments, several in modern spelling like Butler's other decipherments, are
Constable, crown, heir (and heire), place, popishly, nourished, descended, house, sole, daughter, marriage (twice),
young (twice) and Dampuille. "papistes" is not on the page ("popishly" is), and neither is "effected" ("profited" is).
The HathiTrust word data gives no word order, so these are crib candidates, not a reading.

**R7773, f. 60v** (after "but the K. thereuppon saide vnto me"):
> that his faith is not vendible.

**R7774, f. 109v**:
> choise expreslie to negotiate therein, for the difficulties of empeachement wch he knoweth he will geive to
> the same in respect of his owne interest against [£] pretending for the gouernment of Champaigne, [?] hauing
> no desire it should nowe take place, the rather for that he hath renewed his intelligences in Amiens and
> Rheins by the meanes of the duke; the said townes…

f. 110 has no cipher.

**R7775, f. 112**:
> [£2]: execute an enterprise vppon Rheins wch would be the ca[…]inge of the place and him.
Then in clear: "The King hath a disposition to geue him the Gouernment of Prouence to embarque him against the D. Espernon…".

## Print check (30 Sept 2026)

HathiTrust still blocks this machine. `imgsrv` answers "Blocked from HathiTrust" on IP reputation (IPv4 and IPv6),
and `pt`, `pt/search`, `ssd` and `ls` give a Cloudflare challenge. Google Books redirects to its captcha page, which
was not followed. The HathiTrust Research Center *Extracted Features 2.0* file for the volume is open data and was
used instead. It gives per-page word counts from the OCR, for all 532 page images, without word order:
`https://data.analytics.hathitrust.org/features-2020.03/mdp/31891/mdp.39015083993017.json.bz2`.
`butler1913_check.py` downloads it and prints the numbers below.

Butler's source notes ("Stowe MS. 166, f. …") and his running heads identify five of the six letters:

| R7771 | ff. 56, 57b | XXI | 51–54 | Tours, 16 Feb 1593 | f. 57: 7 of 7 on p. 53 (OCR "clamonr", "hopinge") |

As a baseline, no other page of the volume holds more than 5 of the 17 f. 58v words, 5 of the 12 f. 50 words, 2 of
the 7 f. 57 words, 1 of the f. 60v words or 3 of the 5 f. 112 words. Each of these pages also has "cypher" or
"Cypher" (pp. 46, 53, 56, 58, 150). The deciphered words are in modern spelling ("secretly", "learn", "young",
"hands", "under"), while the clear text on the same page keeps the old ("verie", "haue", "wch"). They are garbled
the way the OCR garbles italics. So Butler printed the cipher passages deciphered, apparently in italics, with a note.
His introduction, p. xxix, discusses the cypher: "cypher" occurs twice there, with "key", "blanks", "duplicate(s)",
"Record Office", "Hatfield", "secretary", "Stevenson", "Unton" and "1847" (Stevenson's Unton Correspondence,
Roxburghe Club 1847). Whether Butler deciphered the passages himself or took Burghley's decipherments from the
duplicates cannot be told without the page. p. 57 has "Endorsed", "Threr" and "Symmons", the endorsement behind
DECODE's "John Symmons".

Stevenson's Unton Correspondence (archive.org `cu31924027964471`) prints Unton's 1591 cypher, p. 14. It is a
letters-and-digits alphabet with code names ("Emanuel" = the Queen), not the Stowe 166 sign key.
